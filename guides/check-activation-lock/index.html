How to check Activation Lock on a used Mac before you buy

The short answer

  1. Hold the Option key, click the Apple menu and choose System Information.
  2. Under Hardware Overview, find Activation Lock Status. It should say Disabled.
  3. If it says Enabled, don't pay yet. Ask the seller to turn off Find My in front of you, then check again.

If there's no Activation Lock Status line at all, the Mac doesn't support Activation Lock.

What Activation Lock is

Activation Lock ties a Mac to its owner's Apple Account. It turns on by itself when the owner signs in and turns on Find My. Once the Mac is erased, it can't be set up again without that Apple Account and password, so a locked Mac is of little use to anyone else.

It exists on Macs with Apple silicon or the Apple T2 Security Chip running macOS Catalina or later. That's every Apple-silicon Mac, and the Intel Macs with a T2 chip, most of them from 2018 to 2020 (Apple keeps a list of T2 Macs).

Checking it

System Information: hold Option, click the Apple menu and choose System Information. Hardware Overview, the page it opens on, shows Activation Lock Status: Enabled or Disabled.

Terminal: the same line, typed by hand:

system_profiler SPHardwareDataType | grep "Activation Lock"

Check it on the Mac itself, not from a photo or a screenshot the seller sends. Both are easy to fake.

What the seller has to do

If it says Enabled, the seller turns off Find My in front of you, as Apple describes:

  1. Apple menu › System Settings, then click their name at the top of the sidebar.
  2. Click iCloud, then See All (or Show More Apps), then Find My Mac, and turn it off. macOS asks for their Apple Account password.
  3. Check System Information again. It should now say Disabled.

A seller who can't do this, or has a reason not to (“I forgot the password”, “it's my cousin's”), is telling you the Mac isn't theirs to sell. If the Mac is already erased and asks for someone else's Apple Account during setup, it's locked: only the owner can remove it, from iCloud.com/find.

Before you leave, the seller should also sign out of their Apple Account (System Settings › their name › Sign Out) and, ideally, erase the Mac in front of you (see the checklist).

Activation Lock off isn't the whole story

A Mac can be free of Activation Lock and still belong to a company that will take it back after an erase. That's a separate check: how to check a used Mac for MDM. And no service can remove Activation Lock without the owner: offers to “unlock” or “bypass” it aren't something you want to pay for.
