Selling an Intel Mac after macOS 27

The short answer

macOS 27 runs only on Apple silicon: Apple said at WWDC 2025 that macOS Tahoe 26 is the last major version for Intel Macs. Your Mac keeps working, and security updates usually continue for a while, but buyers will ask. To sell it well:

  1. Back up everything you want to keep.
  2. Make a report of its condition (battery, SSD, locks) before you erase it.
  3. Sign out of your Apple Account, which turns off Find My and Activation Lock.
  4. If it was ever a work Mac, have the organization release it.
  5. Erase it, then list it with the facts buyers check.

What macOS 27 means for your Mac

Only four Intel models run macOS Tahoe 26: the MacBook Pro (16-inch, 2019), the MacBook Pro (13-inch, 2020, Four Thunderbolt 3 ports), the iMac (Retina 5K, 27-inch, 2020) and the Mac Pro (2019). Every other Intel Mac stopped at macOS 15 or earlier. None of them get macOS 27.

Apple doesn't promise how long it keeps fixing security problems in older macOS versions. It has usually kept patching a version for about two years after the next one ships, which for macOS 26 would mean into 2028. Say which macOS the Mac runs in your listing, and that it won't get macOS 27. Buyers find out anyway, and a listing that says so up front reads as honest.

1. Before you erase: record its condition

Once it's erased and at the setup screen, nobody can easily check the battery or SSD. So check first: System Information › Power shows the battery's cycle count and condition, and System Information › Storage shows the SSD's S.M.A.R.T. status. Or run Tirekick in seller mode (below), which checks all of it and makes a report card for your listing.

2. Sign out and turn off Find My

Following Apple's guide to selling a Mac:

  • Apple Account: System Settings › your name › Sign Out (on macOS 12 and earlier, System Preferences › Apple ID). This also turns off Find My and Activation Lock. A Mac that's still locked to you is worth little to anyone else.
  • iMessage: Messages › Settings › iMessage › Sign Out.
  • Bluetooth: unpair the keyboard, mouse or trackpad you're keeping, so they don't connect to the old Mac by mistake.

3. Was it ever a work or school Mac?

If a company or school bought it, it may still be assigned to them in Apple Business or Apple School Manager, even if they gave it to you. After an erase it would show the buyer a Remote Management screen and enroll in their management. Check with the two commands in how to check a Mac for MDM. If either names the organization, ask its IT team to release the Mac before you sell it. Only they can.

4. Erase it

  • Intel Macs with a T2 chip on macOS 12 or later (most 2018–2020 models): System Settings › General › Transfer or Reset › Erase All Content and Settings. It erases everything and keeps macOS installed.
  • Other Intel Macs: start up in macOS Recovery (hold Command-R while it starts), erase the startup disk in Disk Utility, then choose Reinstall macOS. When setup appears, press Command-Q to shut down and leave setup to the buyer. Then reset NVRAM, as Apple's guide describes.

Selling in person? Leave the erase until the meetup and do it in front of the buyer, after they've checked the Mac. Buyers can't check an erased Mac without setting it up first.

5. A listing buyers trust

  • The exact model name from About This Mac, such as “MacBook Pro (13-inch, 2020, Four Thunderbolt 3 ports)”, plus chip, memory and storage.
  • Battery cycle count and condition, and the SSD's S.M.A.R.T. status.
  • The macOS it runs, and that it won't get macOS 27.
  • “Activation Lock off, not managed by any organization”, and that the buyer is welcome to check both.
  • Honest photos of any dents, scratches or screen marks.
