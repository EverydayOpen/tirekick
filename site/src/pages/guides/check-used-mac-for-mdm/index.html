How to check a used Mac for MDM and company assignment before you buy

The short answer

A company can hold on to a Mac in two ways, and you need to check both before you pay:

  1. Managed now. Open Terminal and run profiles status -type enrollment. Both lines should end in No.
  2. Assigned to the company, even when nothing is managed right now. Run sudo profiles show -type enrollment with the Mac online. “Client is not DEP enabled.” means Apple reports no organization has set it to enroll in its device management. On a Mac you don't control, Terminal output can be faked. Running it from a local account you created yourself lowers that risk but doesn't remove it, because some Terminal settings apply to every account. An OrganizationName means one has.

If either one names an organization, walk away unless it's your employer's Mac. Only that organization can release it, and no “MDM bypass” changes that.

Why this matters

Companies and schools buy Macs through Apple Business (called Apple Business Manager until 2026) or Apple School Manager, and assign them to their device management (MDM) service. A Mac assigned this way enrolls itself again every time it's set up, even after a full erase. During setup it shows a Remote Management screen, and Apple says that screen can't be skipped. From then on, the organization can lock, erase or restrict the Mac.

That's how people end up with a Mac that worked fine at the meetup and “belongs to a company” after they erase it. The seller may not even know: a Mac that left a company without being released stays assigned.

Step 1: is it managed right now?

Open Terminal (in Applications › Utilities) and type:

profiles status -type enrollment

No administrator password is needed. You'll see two lines:

LineWhat it means
Enrolled via DEP: NoNot set up through the organization's automated enrollment. Good.
Enrolled via DEP: YesAn organization set this Mac up through Automated Device Enrollment. Walk away.
MDM enrollment: NoNo device management service controls it. Good.
MDM enrollment: YesA device management service controls it (“User Approved” means someone approved it on this Mac). Walk away unless you know and trust the organization.

You can also look in System Settings: on macOS 15 and later, General › Device Management; on macOS 13 and 14, Privacy & Security › Profiles. A profile from an organization you don't know is a reason to ask questions. Apple notes that a profile you can't remove may mean the Mac is managed.

Step 2: is it assigned to a company?

A Mac can pass step 1 and still be assigned: after an erase, or when it was set up without the organization's enrollment, nothing is enrolled, but Apple's servers still list it. Connect the Mac to the internet, open Terminal and type:

sudo profiles show -type enrollment

Type the Mac's administrator password when asked (it doesn't show as you type).

  • Error fetching Device Enrollment configuration: Client is not DEP enabled. Apple reports no organization has set this Mac to enroll in its device management. Good. On a Mac you don't control, Terminal output can be faked. Running it from a local account you created yourself lowers that risk but doesn't remove it, because some Terminal settings apply to every account.
  • Device Enrollment configuration: followed by a block with OrganizationName = … The Mac is assigned to that organization. Walk away, and tell the seller who it belongs to.
  • An error that mentions the cloud configuration server, or anything else: the check didn't work, usually because the Mac is offline. Connect and try again. Don't read an error as “not assigned”.

If a notification then asks you to enroll the Mac in device management, don't. It means the same thing: the Mac is assigned to an organization.

If the Mac has already been erased

Go through setup connected to Wi-Fi or a phone hotspot, and watch the next screens. Apple silicon and T2 Macs that are registered to an organization need the internet to get through setup after an erase. A Remote Management screen naming an organization means walk away. If setup finishes without one, create a local account and run the two commands above.

Why an “MDM bypass” isn't a fix

  • A bypass hides the enrollment screen on one installation. The Mac stays assigned to the organization, so the screen can come back after an update or an erase.
  • The organization can still see and manage the Mac once it enrolls again.
  • A Mac sold “with MDM bypassed” is a Mac the seller couldn't get released. Only the organization can release it, from its Apple Business or Apple School Manager account.

If you already bought one, contact the seller and the organization named in the output, and ask the organization to release the Mac.
